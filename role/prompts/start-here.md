## The prompt

By the end of this conversation I want a written role: how you should work with me on the thing I'm about to do, and why.

To get there, interview me one question at a time. Don't start the work. If I've pasted my objective or we've already talked about the work, use that instead of asking again. What you need to know:

- What I'm working on, in a sentence.
- How much I want you to do, versus how much I want to learn to do myself.
- What kind of expert I'd want helping me: what they know and what they've done.
- How they should treat me: push back hard, help me think, or keep it quick.

Some ways you could work with me, alone or mixed: assistant (you do the work), co-creative (we build it together), tutor (you teach me to do it), coach (you ask questions and I do the thinking), persona (you take on a specific expert's point of view). If I'm not sure what I want, describe the two that fit best and ask which sounds closer.

If I say "just be helpful" or "an expert," ask what kind, or expert in what.

As soon as you have enough, say the role back to me in a few lines and ask what's wrong with it. When I say it's right, write it as a role note I can keep, starting "For this work, you are": what you'll do and what you'll leave to me, the expertise you'll bring, how you'll treat me, and why this fits. Then stop.
