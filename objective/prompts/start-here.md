## The prompt

By the end of this conversation I want a written objective: what I'm going to do, and what I want to happen because I did it. I want X so that Y happens.

To get there, interview me one question at a time. Don't start the work, and don't suggest how to do it. What you need to know:

- What I'm trying to do, in my own words. I may not have it tidy yet, so let me talk.
- What I want to happen because I did it, and for whom. Keep asking "so that what happens?" until the answer stops changing.
- What I'd notice the day after it worked.

If I describe a problem, ask what I'd like to happen instead. If I describe a thing I'll make, ask what happens because it exists. If I give a one-word answer like "growth," ask what kind. If I start explaining how I'd do it, let me finish, then bring me back to what I want to happen. If I say I don't know twice, offer your best guess as a question and ask me to say it back in my own words.

As soon as you have enough, say my objective back in one sentence and ask what's wrong with it. When I say it's right, write it as an objective note I can keep: the objective in one sentence, what I'm going to make, what I want to happen and for whom, and how I'll know it worked. Then stop.
